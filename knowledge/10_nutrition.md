# Nutrition

## Purpose

Nutrition should support the user's primary training goals:

1. Strength and muscle development
2. Improved body composition
3. Running performance and endurance
4. Recovery
5. Long-term health

The agent should not treat nutrition as a separate system from training.

Training load, recovery, body composition, and nutrition must be
considered together.

---

# 1. Energy Balance

Bodyweight change is primarily driven by the relationship between energy
intake and energy expenditure over time.

### Energy surplus

Energy intake consistently exceeds expenditure.

This generally supports weight gain.

### Energy deficit

Energy expenditure consistently exceeds intake.

This generally supports weight loss.

### Energy balance

Intake and expenditure are approximately matched.

Bodyweight tends to remain relatively stable.

Short-term bodyweight changes can also come from:

- Water
- Glycogen
- Food in the digestive system
- Sodium
- Hormonal fluctuations

Therefore, one weigh-in should not determine a nutrition decision.

---

# 2. Primary Body-Composition Goal

The user wants to become leaner while maintaining or improving muscle and
strength.

The preferred approach is generally:

**Moderate calorie deficit + resistance training + sufficient protein**

rather than aggressive dieting.

The agent should prioritize sustainable fat loss over rapid scale-weight
loss.

---

# 3. Aggressive Deficits

Large calorie deficits can negatively affect:

- Training performance
- Recovery
- Strength progression
- Running performance
- Energy
- Hunger
- Adherence
- Muscle retention

The agent should not recommend aggressive restriction simply because faster
weight loss appears attractive.

---

# 4. Protein

Protein is particularly important when resistance training and dieting
are combined.

A practical target for this user is approximately:

**1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**

At approximately 80 kg bodyweight, this corresponds roughly to:

**128–176 g/day**

The exact target does not need to be perfect every day.

Consistency over time matters more than hitting an exact number daily.

---

# 5. Protein Distribution

Protein can be distributed across the day rather than consumed mostly in
one meal.

Useful protein sources include:

- Eggs
- Milk
- Curd/yogurt
- Paneer
- Chicken
- Fish
- Soy
- Dal
- Beans
- Chana
- Whey protein if useful

The agent should consider the user's food preferences, budget, and
dietary constraints.

---

# 6. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are useful for:

- Running
- Higher-volume training
- Glycogen replenishment
- High-intensity exercise
- General training performance

The user should not unnecessarily eliminate carbohydrates while training
three times per week for both running and strength.

Useful sources include:

- Rice
- Roti
- Oats
- Poha
- Potatoes
- Fruits
- Bread
- Other grains

---

# 7. Dietary Fat

Dietary fat supports:

- Energy intake
- Cell function
- Hormonal processes
- Absorption of fat-soluble vitamins

The agent should avoid extremely low-fat diets.

Useful sources include:

- Nuts
- Seeds
- Eggs
- Dairy
- Peanut butter
- Oils
- Fish

---

# 8. Micronutrients

A good diet should provide adequate:

- Vitamins
- Minerals
- Fiber
- Essential fatty acids

The agent should prioritize food quality and variety before suggesting
supplements.

Supplements should not be treated as a substitute for a poor diet.

---

# 9. Fiber

Fiber supports:

- Digestive health
- Satiety
- Food quality

Useful sources include:

- Vegetables
- Fruits
- Dal
- Beans
- Chana
- Whole grains
- Nuts and seeds

Fiber intake should also be considered around running because very high
fiber intake immediately before running can cause gastrointestinal
discomfort for some people.

---

# 10. Hydration

Hydration affects:

- Running performance
- Exercise tolerance
- Recovery
- General well-being

Fluid requirements vary with:

- Temperature
- Humidity
- Sweat rate
- Exercise duration
- Body size
- Daily activity

The agent should avoid prescribing one universal water number regardless
of conditions.

---

# 11. Electrolytes

Electrolyte needs increase when sweat losses are substantial.

Relevant situations include:

- Long runs
- Hot weather
- Heavy sweating
- Prolonged exercise

For ordinary short sessions, a normal balanced diet may provide
sufficient electrolytes.

---

# 12. Pre-Workout Nutrition

A pre-workout meal should provide useful energy without causing
unnecessary digestive discomfort.

Depending on timing, useful options can include:

- Poha
- Banana
- Bread
- Oats
- Rice
- Curd
- Eggs
- Other familiar foods

The closer the meal is to training, the more useful it may be to keep
the meal relatively easy to digest.

---

# 13. Post-Workout Nutrition

There is no requirement to consume a protein shake immediately after
training.

The main priorities are:

- Total daily protein
- Adequate total calories
- Adequate carbohydrates
- Normal meal timing

A protein-containing meal after training is useful, but the entire day's
nutrition matters more than a narrow post-workout window.

---

# 14. Running Nutrition

For shorter easy runs, special fueling is generally unnecessary.

As running duration increases, carbohydrate and fluid needs become more
important.

Longer sessions should consider:

- Pre-run carbohydrate availability
- Hydration
- Electrolytes when appropriate
- Carbohydrate during the session when duration warrants it

The agent should gradually introduce fueling strategies rather than
testing unfamiliar foods during important long runs.

---

# 15. Calorie Tracking

Calories can be tracked when useful.

However, calorie estimates are never perfectly accurate.

Common sources of error include:

- Portion size
- Cooking oil
- Restaurant food
- Label inaccuracies
- Homemade recipes
- Untracked snacks

The agent should use trends rather than pretending that daily calorie
numbers are exact.

---

# 16. Bodyweight Tracking

Bodyweight should ideally be measured under reasonably consistent
conditions.

Useful approach:

- Similar time of day
- Similar clothing
- Similar hydration conditions
- Preferably morning measurements

Daily measurements can be useful, but the agent should evaluate the
trend rather than individual readings.

---

# 17. Weight-Loss Trend

The agent should evaluate weight change over multiple weeks.

If bodyweight is declining gradually and performance is stable:

Continue.

If weight is not changing for a sufficiently long period:

Review actual intake, activity, and tracking accuracy before changing
calories.

If weight is dropping rapidly while performance and recovery deteriorate:

The deficit may be too aggressive.

---

# 18. Waist Measurement

Waist circumference can provide useful body-composition information.

It should be measured consistently.

The agent can compare:

- Bodyweight trend
- Waist trend
- Training performance
- Progress photos if the user chooses to track them

No single metric should determine progress.

---

# 19. Recomposition

Because the user is resistance training while carrying excess body fat,
some improvement in body composition can potentially occur without
dramatic scale-weight changes.

Useful indicators include:

- Waist decreasing
- Strength improving
- Stable or slowly changing bodyweight
- Better muscular development
- Improved fitness

The agent should not assume that a stagnant scale means no progress.

---

# 20. Nutrition and Strength

For strength and muscle development, the agent should prioritize:

- Sufficient protein
- Adequate recovery
- Progressive resistance training
- Adequate energy availability
- Carbohydrates sufficient for training

If strength consistently declines during dieting, the nutrition strategy
should be reviewed.

---

# 21. Nutrition and Running

Running performance depends partly on carbohydrate availability.

If the user repeatedly feels unusually weak during runs, investigate:

- Recent meals
- Total calorie intake
- Carbohydrate intake
- Hydration
- Sleep
- Training load

Do not automatically assume lack of fitness.

---

# 22. Nutrition During Higher Running Volume

When running volume increases, energy expenditure may also increase.

The agent should consider whether additional food is required to maintain
training quality and recovery.

A user trying to lose fat does not need to eat back every calorie burned
by exercise automatically.

However, excessive restriction can undermine the entire training system.

---

# 23. Nutrition During a Strength-Focused Phase

When strength/muscle is the primary priority:

- Protein remains high.
- Calories should support training.
- Carbohydrates should support performance.
- Fat should not be excessively restricted.

If body composition is also a priority, a small-to-moderate deficit can
be used when appropriate.

---

# 24. Nutrition During an Endurance-Focused Phase

When running becomes the primary objective:

- Carbohydrate availability becomes more important.
- Long-run fueling may become necessary.
- Total energy availability should be monitored.
- Protein should remain adequate.
- Aggressive dieting should generally be avoided.

The agent should not force the same calorie strategy across every phase.

---

# 25. Food Quality

A practical diet should contain a variety of:

- Protein sources
- Fruits
- Vegetables
- Whole or minimally processed carbohydrate sources
- Healthy fat sources
- Dairy or appropriate alternatives

Highly processed foods do not automatically need to be eliminated.

The overall diet matters more than individual foods.

---

# 26. Flexible Eating

The system should allow foods the user enjoys.

A sustainable diet does not require perfect food choices every day.

The agent should focus on:

- Total calories
- Protein
- Overall food quality
- Fiber
- Micronutrients
- Training support
- Adherence

---

# 27. Supplements

Supplements should be considered only when they have a clear purpose.

The agent should distinguish between:

### Potentially useful

A supplement with evidence for a specific goal and appropriate use.

### Optional

Potential benefit but not necessary if diet and training are already
adequate.

### Unnecessary

A product being marketed as essential without a meaningful reason.

The agent should not recommend supplements casually.

---

# 28. Food Logging

If the user logs food, useful information includes:

- Food
- Approximate quantity
- Meal timing
- Protein
- Calories when available
- Carbohydrates when relevant
- Fat when relevant
- Fiber when relevant

The system should distinguish measured values from estimates.

---

# 29. Nutrition Data Quality

The agent must not pretend that an image or rough description gives exact
calorie and micronutrient values.

Examples such as:

"one bowl of poha"

or

"a handful of nuts"

have substantial variation.

The agent should provide an estimate range when precision is impossible.

---

# 30. Nutrition and Recovery Signals

Poor nutrition may contribute to:

- Low energy
- Poor training performance
- Increased hunger
- Poor recovery
- Weight changes
- Reduced motivation

These signals should be interpreted alongside sleep and training load.

---

# 31. Decision Rules

### If bodyweight is slowly decreasing + strength is stable:

Continue the current nutrition strategy.

### If bodyweight is decreasing rapidly + performance is falling:

Increase energy intake or reduce the deficit.

### If bodyweight is stable + waist is decreasing + performance is good:

Do not automatically reduce calories.

### If weight is stable + waist is stable over a meaningful period:

Review intake and activity before changing calories.

### If running performance declines during dieting:

Check carbohydrate intake, total calories, hydration, sleep, and training
load.

### If hunger is consistently excessive:

The deficit may be too aggressive or the diet may lack sufficient
satiety.

### If recovery is poor:

Review total energy, protein, carbohydrates, sleep, and training stress.

---

# 32. Core Principle

Nutrition should support the training system rather than fight against it.

For this user, the default strategy is:

**High-quality resistance training + sufficient protein + adequate
carbohydrates + moderate calorie control + sustainable eating.**

The objective is not to become as light as possible.

The objective is to become **leaner, stronger, fitter, and capable of
sustaining the training required to stay that way.**
