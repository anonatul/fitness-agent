# Current Training Phase

## Purpose

This file defines the user's **current training phase**.

The phase determines what the training system emphasizes right now.

A phase is not a fixed calendar period. It continues while it is producing useful progress and recovery remains manageable.

---

# Current Phase

## Phase Name

**Foundation Strength & Hypertrophy + Aerobic Base**

---

## Phase Objective

Build a strong foundation for long-term athletic development by improving:

1. Basic strength
2. Muscle development
3. Movement competence
4. Training tolerance
5. Running consistency
6. Aerobic capacity
7. Recovery habits

The immediate objective is **not** to maximize any single performance metric.

The goal is to establish a sustainable training base from which future phases can build.

---

# Current Priority

### Primary

**Strength and hypertrophy**

The majority of adaptation resources should support resistance-training progress.

Focus on:

- Progressive overload
- Consistent movement patterns
- Appropriate training volume
- ~1–3 RIR for most working sets
- Improving exercise execution
- Increasing strength over time

---

### Secondary

**Body composition**

Support gradual improvement in body composition while preserving training performance.

Avoid aggressive calorie restriction.

---

### Tertiary

**Running and aerobic development**

Maintain approximately three weekly running exposures when recovery allows:

- Easy aerobic run
- Quality-oriented run
- Long easy run

Running should improve aerobic fitness without becoming the dominant training stress.

---

# Current Training Structure

Default structure:

- 3 strength sessions/week
- 3 running sessions/week
- 1 recovery-oriented day

The exact weekly arrangement may change based on:

- Recovery
- Schedule
- Previous training
- Running fatigue
- Strength fatigue
- Pain
- Available time

The structure is a starting framework, not a rigid calendar.

---

# Strength Emphasis

Strength training should emphasize the fundamental movement patterns.

### Upper Body

- Horizontal push
- Horizontal pull
- Vertical push
- Vertical pull

### Lower Body

- Squat
- Single-leg/lunge
- Hip hinge
- Calf work
- Knee-flexion work when practical

### Core

Prioritize:

- Anti-extension
- Anti-rotation
- Lateral stability

---

# Current Strength Development Strategy

The user is currently building basic strength and exercise capacity.

Progression should prioritize:

1. Better technique
2. More quality repetitions
3. Appropriate RIR
4. Increased resistance
5. Harder variations
6. Increased volume when justified

Do not aggressively increase multiple variables simultaneously.

---

# Current Running Strategy

Running is currently focused on building a durable aerobic foundation.

### Easy Run

Purpose:

- Aerobic development
- Recovery-friendly volume
- Running consistency

Intensity:

- Conversational
- Controlled
- Low-to-moderate effort

---

### Quality Run

Purpose:

- Improve ability to sustain higher aerobic effort
- Introduce controlled faster running
- Improve running economy

Quality work should remain controlled.

The user does not need maximal intervals at this stage.

---

### Long Easy Run

Purpose:

- Build endurance
- Improve durability
- Gradually increase comfort at longer distances

The long run should remain predominantly easy.

---

# Half-Marathon Relationship

The half-marathon goal is currently **not the primary training objective**.

The current phase should build the underlying qualities required for future half-marathon development:

- Aerobic base
- Running consistency
- Lower-body durability
- General strength
- Recovery capacity

Specific half-marathon preparation should become a larger priority only when the user intentionally enters an endurance-focused phase.

---

# Body Composition Strategy

The user wants to become leaner while maintaining or increasing muscle.

Current strategy:

- Maintain sufficient protein
- Avoid aggressive calorie restriction
- Continue resistance training
- Maintain running
- Monitor bodyweight trends
- Monitor waist trends
- Monitor strength performance

A successful outcome may involve:

- Lower waist
- Stable or slowly changing bodyweight
- Improved strength
- Improved fitness

Scale weight alone does not determine success.

---

# Recovery Strategy

Recovery is treated as a training variable.

Monitor:

- Sleep
- Energy
- Soreness
- Motivation
- Training performance
- Resting HR trends when available
- Lower-body fatigue
- Joint/tendon discomfort
- Life stress

If recovery deteriorates, training should be modified before performance collapses.

# Current Phase Rules

## Rule 1 — Progress When Ready

Progress an exercise when:

- Technique is stable
- Target reps are achieved
- RIR is appropriate
- Recovery is acceptable
- No meaningful pain is present

---

## Rule 2 — Do Not Force Progression

Do not progress simply because a new week has started.

Maintain the same workload when:

- Performance is still adapting
- Recovery is borderline
- Technique needs improvement
- Recent training load was high
- Running fatigue is elevated

---

## Rule 3 — Protect Recovery

When strength and running compete for recovery resources, protect the primary goal first.

Reduce secondary training stress before unnecessarily sacrificing the primary training stimulus.

---

## Rule 4 — Pain Overrides Progression

Meaningful pain takes priority over progression.

Modify, replace, or stop the relevant movement when appropriate.

Persistent or concerning pain should be evaluated by an appropriate healthcare professional.

---

## Rule 5 — Missed Sessions Are Recalculated

If a workout is missed:

- Do not automatically double the next session
- Do not cram several hard sessions together
- Consider the previous training load
- Consider the next planned session
- Recalculate the best next workout

---

# Phase Progression

The phase should generally move forward when the user demonstrates:

- Consistent training adherence
- Reliable exercise technique
- Measurable strength progression
- Improved running consistency
- Better recovery awareness
- Ability to tolerate current workload
- No persistent training-limiting pain

Progression does not require every metric to improve simultaneously.

---

# Phase Completion

The phase should be reviewed when one or more of the following occur:

- Progress slows meaningfully
- Current workload becomes easy to recover from
- Strength has improved substantially
- Aerobic base has improved substantially
- Body composition has changed enough to reconsider priorities
- The user wants a different emphasis
- A new capability becomes the next logical target
- Recovery problems persist
- Training boredom or exercise limitations become significant

---

# Phase Transition Options

At review, the agent should choose one of:

### Continue

Current phase is working.

Keep the general structure and continue progressing.

### Progress

Current phase remains appropriate but training demand can increase.

### Maintain

The user has achieved the desired adaptation and wants to preserve it while shifting resources elsewhere.

### Deload

Accumulated fatigue requires temporary reduction in training stress.

### Change Emphasis

A different quality should become the primary objective.

Examples:

- Strength/hypertrophy → endurance
- Endurance → strength/hypertrophy
- Development → maintenance
- General training → half-marathon-specific preparation

---

# Phase Decision Hierarchy

When deciding what to emphasize:

1. Safety
2. Current user priority
3. Recovery capacity
4. Recent training load
5. Current performance
6. Long-term goals
7. Available time/equipment
8. Secondary fitness goals

---

# Phase Review Questions

At each meaningful review, evaluate:

### Strength

- Are major movements progressing?
- Is technique improving?
- Is volume recoverable?

### Hypertrophy

- Is training providing sufficient stimulus?
- Is bodyweight/nutrition supporting the goal?
- Is muscle development progressing?

### Running

- Is running becoming more comfortable?
- Is aerobic capacity improving?
- Is long-run tolerance increasing?
- Is running interfering with strength recovery?

### Recovery

- Is fatigue manageable?
- Is sleep adequate?
- Are soreness and pain controlled?

### Body Composition

- Is waist changing?
- Is bodyweight trend appropriate?
- Is performance being preserved?

### Adherence

- Is the user consistently completing the program?
- Is the program practical with their schedule?

---

# Phase Transition Principle

Do not change phases because a predetermined number of weeks has passed.

Change phases because:

- The goal changed
- Adaptation occurred
- Progress stalled
- Recovery demands changed
- A new capability needs development
- The current emphasis is no longer the best use of training resources

---

# Core Principle

**A training phase exists to concentrate adaptation toward the user's most important current objective while maintaining useful levels of other fitness qualities. The phase changes when the training problem changes—not simply because the calendar says it should.**
